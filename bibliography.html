    https://openlibrary.org/works/OL3671290W — accessed 2026-11-15.

    USED FOR Ritual as a sign system; the critique of reading rites as survivals of earlier religion.

    LIMITATION Theoretical; used here for the critique, not for regional description.

    PLATE A · PLATE C · PLATE E

  2. S02 PRIMARY

    Barsov, Elpidifor Vasil’evich Prichitaniia Severnogo kraia [Laments of the Northern Land]. Moscow, 1872–1886. 3 volumes.

    https://openlibrary.org/search?q=Prichitaniia+Severnogo+kraia — accessed 2026-11-15.

    USED FOR The foundational collection of Russian funeral laments, recorded in the Olonets region; the performer/repertoire model of lament.

    LIMITATION No text is quoted in this atlas; the collection was used for its structure and circumstances of recording, not for verbatim citation.

    PLATE C · PLATE G

  3. S03 MONOGRAPH

    Etkind, Alexander Warped Mourning: Stories of the Undead in the Land of the Unburied. Stanford University Press, Stanford, 2013.

    https://openlibrary.org/works/OL17577534W — accessed 2026-11-15.

    USED FOR The argument that Soviet culture was shaped by improperly buried dead and that Russian literature returns to them as the undead.

    LIMITATION A cultural-historical argument, presented here as interpretation.

    PLATE I · PLATE L

  4. S04 MONOGRAPH

    Ivanits, Linda J. Russian Folk Belief. M. E. Sharpe, Armonk, NY, 1989.

    https://openlibrary.org/works/OL4982500W — accessed 2026-11-15.

    USED FOR Folk beliefs about the soul, the dead, household spirits and the unclean dead; the general account of funeral and memorial custom used in plates A–D.

    LIMITATION A survey of the published ethnographic literature, not a regional field study.

    PLATE A · PLATE B · PLATE D · PLATE E · PLATE G · PLATE H

    USED FOR Twentieth-century death, mourning and memory in Russia; the argument that public and private mourning diverged. Used for interpretation, not as a source of statistics.

    LIMITATION A nineteenth-century handbook; it describes the normative rite, not actual household practice.

  19. S19 MONOGRAPH

    Zelenin, Dmitrii Konstantinovich Ocherki russkoi mifologii: umershie neestestvennoiu smert’iu i rusalki [Essays on Russian Mythology: Those Who Died an Unnatural Death, and the Rusalki]. Tipografiia A. V. Orlova, Petrograd, 1916.

    https://openlibrary.org/works/OL33226560W — accessed 2026-11-15.

    USED FOR The category of the ‘unclean’ dead (zalozhnye pokoiniki), separate burial, and the rusalka hypothesis.

    LIMITATION A synthesis of scattered reports; the category is the author's construction. Later scholarship disputes its coherence and the rusalka derivation.

WHAT IS NOT CITED

Unverified claims are named in the plates as unverified. The atlas does not have a category of ‘common knowledge’ that exempts a claim from citation.
