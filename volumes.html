A plate is published when its sources have been checked. Planned plates are listed so that the scope is visible, and they are marked as research leads, not as content.

VOL. I · PUBLISHED

ARCHÆOLOGY OF RUSSIAN DEATH

Threshold, body, ritual, table, grave, voice, afterlife, collective death, art, the Soviet afterlife, the archive

The first volume: how people in Russia handled the dead, imagined what came after, and tried to keep a record of it.

  1. A Death as a Threshold The ethnography of death in Russia is rarely an account of dying. It is an account of what the living do in the room afterwards — and of how quickly that room becomes a border.
  2. B The Body Before it becomes a memory, the dead is an object that must be handled. The handling is the ritual.
  3. C The Ritual The most confidently repeated ‘Russian funeral custom’ is also the one most often misdescribed: the commemoration on the third, ninth and fortieth day is a liturgical scheme before it is a folk one.
  4. D The Table The memorial meal is the place where the dead is most directly treated as a person with preferences, a place and an appetite.
  5. E The Returning Dead Not every dead person was thought to stay dead. The distinction was not good and evil but proper and improper death — and the category that names it has been under dispute for a century.
  6. F The Grave The Russian grave is a legal object before it is a sacred one. Where the dead may lie has been decided by decrees, epidemics and municipal budgets at least as often as by custom.
  7. G The Voice The dead are spoken to. The grammar of that address is one of the most precisely recorded things in Russian ethnography — and one of the least often quoted accurately.
  8. H The Afterlife Orthodox teaching about the dead is precise, and it is not the same thing as the crowded cosmology of the icon. The atlas keeps the two apart, then puts them on the same page.
  9. I Collective Death Mass death produces two things at once: a hole in the record and an official number. The atlas is interested in the gap between them.
  10. J Death in Art Russian art tends to approach death through a single object held at an angle: a coffin on a sled, a square on a wall, a body on a bed.
  11. K The Soviet and Post-Soviet Afterlife The Soviet state kept the funeral and rebuilt it as a ceremony of the collective; then the collective dissolved.
  12. L The Unfinished Archive This plate is mostly empty on purpose: an atlas of death that showed everything would be lying about the record.

VOL. II · IN PREPARATION

THE CHTHONIC LANDSCAPE

Houses, forests, waters, roads, villages and cellars as places where the living and the dead meet

The second volume moves from the grave to the ground around it. Plates are published one at a time; the rest are listed with their scope and stay unpublished until their sources are checked.

  1. M The Empty House In the Eastern Slavic funeral material the house takes part in a death: its windows are opened, its boundaries are said to break. Decades later the state counts villages with no one in them. The two bodies of evidence are different, and this plate keeps them apart.
  2. N The Forest Edge Three kinds of evidence put the dead near the forest: reports of burial in forest ravines, a belief that the way to the dead leads through the woods, and a fairy-tale hut at the treeline. They come from different peoples and different genres, and the plate does not blend them.
  3. O Water, Marsh and River In the sources read for this plate, water is the place of the drowned, and the swamp is a border. A calendar day remembered those who died by water; an essay reads swamps as a threshold. The plate keeps the dated custom and the interpretation apart.
  4. P Roads and Crossroads A crossroads is where a village ends, where relatives are seen off, and, in one scholar’s reconstruction, where some of the dead were once buried. A modern field count of roadside memorials gives the plate something firmer: 299 signs on three highways. The plate keeps the old reconstruction and the new count apart.
  5. Q The Vanished Village Villages leave the map in two ways: the state floods them, or people leave. The Rybinsk Reservoir covered a town and hundreds of villages; the census records thousands of villages with no residents at all. The plate puts the two side by side and says what is and is not known about the dead who stayed.

VOL. III · PLANNED

THE UNSEEN

Household spirits, the unquiet dead, the calendar of the dead, taboo and protection

Planned. Every figure and custom will carry its region, source, period and terminology, and none will be offered as a unified pantheon.

  1. SHousehold Spirits and Dangerous SpacesDomovoi, bannik, ovinnik and the rules of baths, barns, mills and ovens, recorded region by region.RESEARCH LEAD
  2. TThe Unquiet DeadZalozhnye pokoiniki, upyr’, unnatural death and unfulfilled funerary obligation; folklore kept apart from later literary vampires.RESEARCH LEAD
  3. UThe Calendar of the DeadRadonitsa, parental Saturdays, Semik, Sviatki; church practice, folk custom and later reinterpretation told apart.RESEARCH LEAD
  4. VTaboo, Omen and ProtectionProhibitions, signs, washing and cleansing, protective objects; what people did and what researchers think it meant.RESEARCH LEAD

VOL. IV · PLANNED

MEMORY, CATASTROPHE AND MODERNITY

Abandonment as material process; the digital afterlife

Planned, with comparative dossiers on Old Believer, Muslim, Jewish, Buddhist and northern and Siberian Indigenous funerary traditions, each in its own context.

  1. WThe Materials of AbandonmentRuined houses, neglected cemeteries, demolished churches, reused gravestones; physical decay tied to historical process.RESEARCH LEAD
  2. XThe Digital AfterlifeOnline memorials, profiles of the dead, digital inheritance; treated as a serious contemporary extension, not a novelty.RESEARCH LEAD
