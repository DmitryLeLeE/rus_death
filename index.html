ARCHÆOLOGY of RUSSIAN DEATH

Volume I of the Russian Chthonic Atlas — death, memory, ritual and the afterlife in Russian culture

roofed cross → plate F

first edition 2026 · 17 plates

This is not a history of a single tradition. It is an assembly of documents, objects and arguments about how people in Russia buried their dead, imagined what came after, and tried to keep names from disappearing.

The plates move from the room where a death occurs to the archive that cannot hold it. Each plate has its own evidence and its own gaps. Facts are separated from interpretation and from reconstruction wherever they appear.

No invented sources. No invented quotations. No invented archive numbers.
