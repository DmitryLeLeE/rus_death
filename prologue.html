BEFORETHE FIRSTPLATE

A short note on what this atlas is, what it will not do, and how to move through it.

Russian death is not one thing.

01 · A NAME, NOT AN ESSENCE

The title names a field of evidence, not a national character. What survives is a set of documents about particular places and times: a village recorded by a folklorist, a parish regulated by a synod, a cemetery administered by a city, a memorial built by a state.

These documents overlap and they disagree. The atlas keeps the disagreement on the page instead of averaging it into a custom.

A coffin, a cloth, a bowl of grain, a stone.

02 · MATTER AS DOCUMENT

The plates treat things as documents: what they were made of, who handled them, what they were for and who wrote about them. A photograph is a record of someone’s decision to photograph as much as it is a record of a grave.

Every image carries its credit and its licence. Every image also carries a button that shows the file as it was before the atlas touched it: the damage you see is ours, and it is declared.

Where the evidence stops, the sheet stays empty.

03 · THE EMPTY SHEET

Plate L is almost white on purpose. It lists what is missing — a field survey of a provincial cemetery, a municipal burial register, a scholarly account of post-Soviet digital memorials — and draws the gap instead of filling it.

Nothing here is invented to close a hole: no quotations, no archive numbers, no photographs staged as history. One photograph from Ukraine appears once, and is labelled as Ukrainian.

Three ways in

  • A→LIn a linePlates A to L, from the room where a death occurs to the archive that cannot hold it.
  • ✳In a netThe index crosses plates with objects, concepts and places; the words page lists the vocabulary.
  • ◎On the groundThe map and the chronology pin each claim to a place or a date — or refuse to.

The aesthetic is not darkness. It is Russian death, remembered through matter.
